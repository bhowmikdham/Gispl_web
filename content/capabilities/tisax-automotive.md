TISAX — the Trusted Information Security Assessment Exchange — is the automotive industry's mechanism for checking that suppliers protect information properly. It was created because every OEM used to audit every supplier separately: the German Association of the Automotive Industry (VDA) standardised the questionnaire as the **VDA ISA catalogue**, and the ENX Association built the exchange on which one assessment result can be shared with every customer who needs it. A label is valid for three years and is now a condition of doing business with the German OEMs, most European ones, and a growing number of manufacturers in Japan and the United States.

The catalogue covers information security in depth — organisation, policies, asset management, identity and access, physical security, supplier management, incident handling, business continuity — and adds two objectives that ordinary standards do not: **prototype protection**, for anyone who handles pre-launch vehicles, parts or data, and **data protection** against the GDPR. Each control is scored on a maturity scale, and the target maturity is set by the assessment level the customer requires.

## What the assessment tests

TISAX assessments are performed by ENX-accredited audit providers, at one of three levels. AL1 is a self-assessment. AL2 adds a plausibility check of the self-assessment, usually by video. AL3 — required for very high protection needs and prototype protection — is an on-site assessment with interviews, inspection and evidence review. The assessor tests your self-assessment against reality, so the discipline is in scoring honestly and closing the gaps before the assessment, not in presenting well on the day.

GISPL takes automotive suppliers, engineering firms and IT providers from scoping to label: choosing the objectives and level from what customers have actually asked for, working through the catalogue with your teams, remediating to the target maturity — building on an existing ISO 27001 ISMS where there is one — preparing sites for AL3, and supporting the assessment itself. For Indian suppliers competing for European programmes, the label is the difference between being considered and being screened out.
