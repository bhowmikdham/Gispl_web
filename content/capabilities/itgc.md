IT General Controls are the controls over the environment in which financial systems run: who can access applications and data, how changes to them are made and approved, how operations such as batch jobs, interfaces and backups are run and monitored, and how new systems are built and implemented. They are "general" because every application control — every automated approval, calculation, reconciliation and system-generated report — depends on them. If access is uncontrolled or changes are unmanaged, the auditor cannot trust anything the system produces.

That dependency is why ITGC sit in every financial audit. Indian statutory auditors test them as the foundation of reporting on internal financial controls under the Companies Act. US-listed groups and their subsidiaries face the same under SOX 404, with the added consequence that a material weakness is disclosed to the market. Banks and NBFCs meet them again in RBI's information-systems audit. Service providers whose systems affect customers' financial reporting meet them in SOC 1.

## Where ITGC fail

The failures are consistent across industries: shared and generic accounts; privileged access that was never reviewed; developers who can move their own changes to production; segregation-of-duties conflicts in the ERP that let one person create a vendor and pay it; job failures nobody monitors; backups nobody restores. None of these is hard to fix. What is hard is fixing them in the middle of an audit, without evidence, with the year-end deadline approaching.

GISPL's ITGC engagements start from the financial statements and trace back to the systems that produce them, assess the controls the way the auditor will, remediate with IT and finance together, and build the evidence programme so the following year's request list is answered from a folder. For ERP implementations, we design the controls in before go-live — which costs a fraction of what retrofitting them after the first audit does.
