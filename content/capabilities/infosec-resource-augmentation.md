Most security failures are capacity failures. The organisation knows what it should do — the regulator has said so, the audit has said so, the last assessment said so — and does not have the people to do it. Senior security hires in India take six to nine months, cost more than most budgets allow, and are gone again in two years. In the meantime the CISO role required by RBI, SEBI or IRDAI is unfilled, the ISO programme slips, the remediation plan ages, and the board has no one to ask.

Resource augmentation closes that gap with people rather than promises. GISPL places its own practitioners — the people who deliver our testing, compliance, SOC and forensics work — inside client teams, in the model that fits: a **virtual CISO** for a defined fraction of the week, with the board reporting and regulator-facing responsibilities the role carries; **embedded specialists** — security architects, GRC and compliance officers, SOC analysts, penetration testers, OT engineers, privacy leads — full-time or part-time for a fixed term; **project surge** teams for a certification, a remediation programme or a migration; or **interim leadership** while you hire.

## Why the firm behind the person matters

A placed individual brings the firm's methods and the firm's bench. When the embedded compliance officer needs a penetration test, the testing practice does it. When the vCISO faces an incident, the forensics practice is on the call. When the project needs three more people for six weeks, they exist. That is the difference between a contractor and an augmentation engagement: you get one person and a practice, and the handover at the end is planned from the start.

Every placement begins with a definition of the gap — which decisions, deliverables and coverage are missing, for how long, at what seniority — and a 30-60-90 day plan tied to your regulatory calendar or programme milestones, so the engagement produces outcomes from the first month rather than a settling-in period.
