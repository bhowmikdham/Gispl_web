- q: Do we need SOC 1 or SOC 2?
  a: SOC 1 is about controls relevant to your customers' financial reporting — payroll, fund accounting, claims processing.   a: Type I reports on the design of controls at a point in time; Type II reports on their operation over a period, usually six to twelve months. Customers increasingly ask for Type II. A first-year Type I followed by a Type II is a common path and gives you a report to show while the period runs.

A System and Organization Controls report is an independent auditor's opinion on the controls a service organisation operates for its customers. It exists because customers cannot audit every vendor themselves: instead, one CPA firm examines the vendor's controls and issues a report that every customer — and every customer's auditor — can rely on. In practice it has become the passport for selling to enterprises, and increasingly to mid-market buyers, in North America, Europe and Australia.

**SOC 1** covers controls relevant to your customers' financial reporting. If you run payroll, process claims, administer funds or operate a billing platform, your customers' auditors need it. **SOC 2** covers the systems themselves, against the AICPA's Trust Services Criteria: Security (always in scope) plus, as you choose, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality and Privacy. Both come as **Type I** — control design at a point in time — or **Type II** — controls operating over a period, which is what most customers now ask for.

## Why readiness decides the outcome

A SOC report is not pass or fail. It is an opinion plus a list of every control tested and every exception found — and that list goes to every customer who receives the report. The organisations that get a clean first report are the ones that treated the observation period as something to prepare for: controls designed to be operable, evidence collection scheduled and owned, and someone checking each quarter that nothing slipped. The ones that hand the auditor a policy binder and hope get a first-year report they spend the second year explaining.

GISPL's role is the readiness half: scoping the report to what your customers rely on, designing controls that your teams can actually run, building the evidence programme, and standing beside you through the audit. The report itself is issued by a licensed CPA firm — as it must be — and we work with yours, or introduce one.
