Establishing that money was taken is half an investigation. The other half is finding where it went — through which accounts, which entities, which jurisdictions, to which beneficiary — and whether it can be brought back. Money-trail analysis is that half: the reconstruction of a flow of funds, hop by hop, from source to where it came to rest, with the evidence for every step and a map of what can be recovered.

The trails are rarely simple. Borrowed funds that should have built a plant pass through a supplier that is a related party, into a shell with a nominee director, out to a personal account, and abroad. Fraud proceeds are layered through a dozen accounts in a day. Embezzled money is round-tripped back into the company as "sales". Following them requires analysing full transaction populations rather than samples; resolving counterparties to real entities and people through corporate registries, beneficial-ownership records, addresses and digital evidence; and visualising the result so a committee of creditors, a judge or a board can follow it.

## Who commissions it

Lenders, most often. RBI's framework on frauds and its wilful-defaulter directions require banks and NBFCs to establish diversion or siphoning of funds with evidence before classifying and acting; a documented money trail is that evidence, and consortiums commission it jointly. Resolution professionals, who must identify preferential, undervalued and fraudulent transactions for the committee of creditors. Boards, following a forensic audit that found money leaving. Insurers and counsel, for civil recovery, freezing applications and criminal complaint. And, with increasing urgency, victims of cyber fraud — a diverted payment, an account takeover — where the first hops must be traced within hours to freeze the proceeds in the mule accounts before they move on.

GISPL's money-trail practice works alongside our forensic audit and digital-forensics teams, traces across borders through our own offices and vetted partners within each jurisdiction's law, and evidences every hop to a standard that has supported freezing orders, insolvency proceedings and prosecutions. The report ends with a recovery map — where the money is, who holds it, and how to reach it — because that is the question the client actually asked.
