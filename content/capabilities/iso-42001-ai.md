ISO/IEC 42001 is the international standard for an artificial intelligence management system — the first certifiable framework for governing how an organisation develops, provides and uses AI. Published in December 2023, it follows the same harmonised structure as ISO 27001: leadership, policy, risk assessment, controls, monitoring, audit and improvement. What it adds is specific to AI — an **AI impact assessment** that considers the effects of a system on individuals and society, not just on the organisation; controls across the AI lifecycle from data provenance to decommissioning; requirements for transparency, human oversight and the handling of AI incidents; and governance of the AI an organisation buys as well as the AI it builds.

The standard arrived as regulation did. The European Union's AI Act imposes obligations on providers and deployers of AI systems — reaching non-EU companies that place systems on the EU market — with penalties up to 7% of worldwide turnover. Financial and healthcare regulators are issuing AI-specific expectations. Enterprise customers have added AI governance to due diligence. And the incidents have begun: models that discriminate, assistants that leak data, agents manipulated by prompt injection, staff pasting customer data into public tools. In each case the first question asked is how the AI was governed, and an organisation without a management system has no answer.

## What a working AIMS looks like

An inventory of every AI system in use, with its purpose, its role and its risk. A policy that says what the organisation will and will not do with AI, signed by top management and owned by named people. An impact assessment run before a system is built or bought, and again when it materially changes. Controls over the data that trains and feeds models, over how they are tested and validated, over who approves deployment and how the system is monitored in use. Oversight of the foundation models and AI-enabled products the organisation buys. And an incident process that knows what an AI incident is.
