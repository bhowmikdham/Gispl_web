ISO/IEC 20000-1 is the international standard for a service management system: the framework through which an organisation plans, delivers, measures and improves the IT services it provides — to external customers or to its own business units. It is the certifiable counterpart to ITIL's best-practice guidance, the credential that IT and managed-service tenders in India and abroad now list alongside ISO 27001, and, day to day, the discipline that stops the same outage happening twice.

The standard's requirements cover the whole service lifecycle: a service catalogue and agreed service levels; relationship management with customers and suppliers; capacity, demand and budgeting; the design and transition of new and changed services; incident, service-request, problem and change management; configuration and release; availability and continuity; and the measurement, reporting and review that close the loop. Since the 2018 edition it shares the harmonised structure with ISO 27001 and ISO 22301, which is the point: change control, incident management, configuration and supplier governance are the same processes in all three, and should be built once.

## Where service management meets security

Every security audit we conduct finds the same thing at the top of the list: uncontrolled change. Unapproved firewall rules, undocumented production changes, emergency fixes that were never reviewed. Change management is a service-management process, and it is the one place where ITSM and security are the same discipline. The same is true of incident management — a security incident is an incident — and of supplier management, where a sub-contracted service component is both a delivery risk and a security one.

GISPL designs service management systems that fit the tools and teams you have, aligned to ITIL 4 and integrated with the ISO 27001 and 22301 systems most of our clients also hold. The result is one management system with one audit cycle, service levels that are measured rather than argued, and processes that exist in practice — in the tool, in the reviews, in the reporting — not only in a document written for the certification body.
