Some matters cannot be handled through the normal channels. A whistle-blower alleges a kickback in procurement, and the people who would investigate are the people involved. A competitor is quoting your unpublished pricing, and the leak could be any of forty people. Counterfeits of your product are on the market, and the distributor swears ignorance. A counterparty you are about to sign with has a history nobody has checked. An executive is being threatened, and the police route is not yet the right one. Corporate investigation is the discipline of answering such questions discreetly, lawfully and with evidence that supports whatever action follows.

The constraint that defines good investigation is lawfulness. Pretexting, unauthorised access to data, surveillance beyond what is proportionate — these produce findings that cannot be used and liabilities that can. Every GISPL investigation begins with a mandate agreed with counsel: the question, the intended use of the findings, the legal framework, and the methods that will and will not be used. Within it, the work draws on open-source and records research, digital forensics, field verification, lawful observation and interviews, sequenced so each step is informed by the last and every conclusion is corroborated before it is drawn.

## Independence and discretion

Internal investigations are not believed when the investigators have a stake in the outcome, and they are rarely conducted by people trained to preserve evidence or to interview to a defensible standard. GISPL provides both the independence and the method, and reports in a form that supports the intended action — disciplinary, civil, criminal or commercial — with support for what follows: evidence packs for counsel, coordination with law enforcement, witness statements and testimony. Investigations are conducted from our offices in India, the UAE, the USA, Canada and Australia and through vetted partners elsewhere, and the subject is typically unaware until the organisation chooses to act. The most valuable finding is often the last one in the report: how the matter became possible, and what closes it.
