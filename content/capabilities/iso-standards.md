The International Organization for Standardization publishes the management-system standards that most of the world uses to demonstrate that security, privacy, continuity, service delivery and — most recently — artificial intelligence are governed rather than improvised. Each is certifiable by an accredited body, each follows the same harmonised structure, and each answers a specific question a customer or regulator is asking. Knowing which one answers which question is where most programmes should start.

**ISO/IEC 27001** is the foundation: an information-security management system with a risk assessment, a Statement of Applicability and, in the 2022 edition, 93 controls in four themes. **ISO/IEC 27017** and **27018** extend it into cloud services and the protection of personal data in the public cloud. **ISO/IEC 27701** extends it into a privacy information management system that maps to the GDPR and the DPDP Act. **ISO 27799** applies its controls to health information. **ISO/IEC 42001** is the AI management-system standard, the first certifiable framework for governing AI. **ISO 22301** is business continuity. **ISO/IEC 20000-1** is IT service management.

## One system, several certificates

Because every one of these standards shares the harmonised structure — context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, improvement — an organisation that needs three of them should build one management system, not three. The common clauses are built once; the standard-specific requirements are added; the internal audit, management review and certification audit run as one cycle. Organisations that arrive with three certificates from three consultants and three overlapping documentation sets are spending twice what they need to and drifting between audits.
