The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India regulates the cyber security of every insurer and every regulated intermediary through its **Information and Cyber Security Guidelines**, reissued in 2023 to replace the 2017 framework and to extend its reach across the distribution and servicing chain. The guidelines prescribe governance — a board-level information-security committee, a Chief Information Security Officer, approved policies — an information-security management system, a detailed set of technical and operational controls, requirements for cloud and third parties, a cyber crisis management plan, and an annual cyber security audit and vulnerability assessment by CERT-In empanelled auditors.

Insurance is a demanding sector to secure. Policyholder data combines health, financial and identity information; distribution runs through thousands of agents, brokers and aggregators; claims processing involves hospitals, garages and third-party administrators; and the sector's digital channels are a standing target for fraud and account takeover. The guidelines exist because the regulator understands that, and they hold the insurer accountable for the chain, not just for its own systems.

## What compliance looks like in practice

IRDAI, like RBI, inspects rather than certifies. The annual audit is the primary evidence, and the board's compliance certificate rests on it. Our engagements build the programme the audit examines: the governance structures, an ISMS aligned to ISO 27001 so that one management system serves the regulator and your insurer or partner audits alike, the technical controls, the crisis management plan, and the empanelled audit and testing themselves. For intermediaries and insurtechs, the engagement is scoped to the requirements that bind your entity and to what your insurer partners will look for when they audit you — which, increasingly, they do.
