The Reserve Bank of India regulates the technology and cyber security of every bank, non-banking financial company, housing financier and payment system operator in the country, and it does so in detail. The centrepiece is the **Master Direction on Information Technology Governance, Risk, Controls and Assurance Practices**, issued in November 2023 and in force from April 2024, which consolidated a decade of circulars into one framework and made the board personally accountable for IT and cyber-risk oversight. Around it sit the **Cyber Security Framework for Banks** of 2016, still the operative cyber baseline; the **Master Direction on Outsourcing of IT Services**, which makes you accountable for what your vendors do; the **Digital Payment Security Controls** directions; and the tiered cyber security requirements for cooperative banks.

What all of them share is a supervisory model. RBI does not certify; it inspects. Its inspectors arrive with the directions and a request list, and the outcome depends on whether the governance exists, whether the controls are in place, whether an independent empanelled auditor has tested them, and whether the evidence is to hand. Penalties are published by name. Business restrictions — stopping a bank from onboarding customers or issuing cards until the deficiencies are fixed — have been used against some of the largest institutions in the country.

## What the directions actually require

Governance first: an IT Strategy Committee of the board, an IT Steering Committee, a CISO with an independent reporting line, board-approved policies and a reporting cadence. Then risk and controls: an inventory of every IT asset, risk assessment, access management, network and data security, change management, vendor and outsourcing risk, business continuity. Then assurance: annual vulnerability assessment and penetration testing and an information-systems audit, both by CERT-In empanelled organisations, with findings tracked to closure. And throughout, incident management: a cyber crisis management plan and reporting of incidents to CERT-In and RBI within six hours.
