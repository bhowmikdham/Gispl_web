ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard for an information-security management system: a defined, risk-based, continually improved way of protecting information, which an independent accredited body can audit and certify. It is the most widely recognised security credential in the world, the one customers and tenders ask for by name, and the accepted evidence that an organisation has the management system regulators from RBI to SEBI expect.

The standard has two halves. The main clauses require the system itself: understanding the organisation's context, leadership commitment, a risk assessment and treatment process, resources and competence, documented operation, performance evaluation and improvement. **Annex A** lists 93 reference controls — in the 2022 edition, grouped into organisational, people, physical and technological themes — and the **Statement of Applicability** records which of them the organisation has adopted and why the rest do not apply.

## Certificate versus system

The most common failure in ISO 27001 is an organisation that wants the certificate more than the system. It is possible to pass a certification audit with documentation written for the auditor and controls that exist on paper; it is not possible to survive the first serious incident, or the first surveillance audit by a diligent auditor, that way. And a certificate obtained that way costs nearly as much as one obtained properly, because the work is the same — it has simply been done badly.

GISPL builds systems that run. Scope is defined tightly enough to be maintained and widely enough to cover what matters. The risk assessment uses a method your own team can repeat. Policies are short and owned by the people who follow them. Internal audit and management review happen before the certification body arrives, not as a formality after. And maintenance is a programme — the annual cycle run continuously — because most lapsed certificates lapse for want of someone owning the year between audits.
