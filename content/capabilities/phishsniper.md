Phishing changed, and most simulation platforms did not. The attacks that reach inboxes today are fluent, personalised and specific — the supplier whose bank details changed, the chief executive who needs the payment approved before the flight, the IT notice referencing the system you actually use — increasingly written with the same generative tools defenders talk about. Simulations built from misspelt templates train people to recognise a threat that has largely disappeared, and a low click rate against them is evidence of nothing.

PhishSniper™ is GISPL's answer: a phishing and social-engineering simulation platform built on generative AI to produce campaigns at the realism of current attacks, in the organisation's own context — its suppliers, its systems, its internal language — across the scenarios that actually cause loss: credential harvesting, malicious attachments, business-email compromise, executive impersonation, supplier fraud, QR-code lures and MFA-fatigue. Every campaign is reviewed before it is sent, and every campaign is practice rather than a trap: an immediate, brief learning moment for those who click, recognition for those who report, no naming and no shaming.

## Measured by the metric that matters

Click rate is the number everyone quotes; report rate and time to report are the numbers that change outcomes, because a user who reports a phish within minutes has given the SOC the earliest detection it will get. PhishSniper™ tracks both by department, role, location and scenario, identifies repeat-clicker cohorts for targeted follow-up, and produces the trend over time that a board can follow — the human-risk metric most organisations lack. Results feed back into gateway tuning and awareness content, so the technical and human defences are managed as one programme.

The platform is delivered managed by GISPL as part of a security-awareness programme, or licensed to security teams with our campaign library and support. It produces the records that RBI, SEBI, IRDAI and ISO 27001 expect for simulated phishing — and, more usefully, the evidence of whether the people are getting better.
