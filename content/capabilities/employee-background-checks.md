The most consequential security decision most organisations make is whom they hire. Insider incidents — fraud, data theft, sabotage, collusion — are committed disproportionately by people whose histories would have raised a flag: a fabricated degree, an undisclosed dismissal for cause, a criminal matter, a directorship in a competitor. In Indian hiring markets, discrepancies between a CV and the verifiable record are common enough that a screened workforce is a materially different population from an unscreened one. Regulators know this — RBI, SEBI and IRDAI expect screening for sensitive roles — and ISO 27001 requires it for everyone.

Screening is only useful if it is lawful, proportionate and fast. Lawful means candidate notice and consent under the DPDP Act, checks proportionate to the role, and handling of the personal data involved to the standard the Act requires — an unlawful check produces a finding that cannot be used and a liability that can. Proportionate means a role matrix: a privileged administrator, a cashier and a marketing intern do not need the same package. Fast means turnaround measured against the offer letter, because screening that delays hiring is screening that gets skipped.

## How GISPL runs it

A policy and role matrix designed with HR and legal. Consent and collection through a managed portal, once per candidate. Verification at source — institutions, employers, court records at the relevant jurisdictions, registries, sanctions lists — by our verification team, with evidence retained. Discrepancies confirmed, the candidate given the chance to explain, and the finding adjudicated against criteria agreed in advance rather than left to a recruiter's instinct. Reports per candidate within five to ten working days, expedited for critical hires, and scaled to campus intakes in the thousands. And for privileged and regulated roles, periodic re-screening — because the person who was clean at hiring is not necessarily clean five years later, and the regulator will ask.
