"How secure are we?" is the question every board asks and every security leader struggles to answer in a form that can be compared year on year. Vulnerability counts do not answer it. Certificates answer a narrower question. A maturity assessment answers it directly: across every domain of a security programme — governance, risk management, identity, protection, detection, response, recovery, third parties — how well managed is the capability, on a defined scale, with evidence, and where does it need to be?

The distinction between compliance and maturity matters. An organisation can hold every certificate its regulators require and still run a programme that is ad hoc: controls that exist because an auditor asked, incidents handled by whoever is available, no measurement, no improvement loop. Such organisations pass audits and suffer breaches. Maturity is about whether the programme is repeatable, measured and improving — and it is the property that predicts outcomes.

## From score to roadmap

The assessment is evidence-based: interviews across leadership and teams, document review and technical samples, scored against the chosen framework — NIST CSF 2.0's tiers, CIS Controls implementation groups, C2M2 for critical infrastructure, or a regulator's baseline. The current-state profile shows where the programme is strong and where it is not. The target state is set from risk appetite, regulatory obligation and business context, per domain, because "level four everywhere" is not a strategy. And the gap becomes a roadmap: initiatives sequenced by risk reduction per rupee, with owners, effort, cost and dependencies, in phases the board can approve.

GISPL's maturity assessments are most often commissioned by new security leaders who need a baseline and a first-year plan, by boards setting budgets, by groups comparing subsidiaries on one scale, and by regulated entities that would rather find the inspection findings themselves. The re-assessment a year later is what turns the exercise into a measurement — and the trajectory is what boards fund.
