A forensic audit is an investigation, not an opinion. Where a statutory audit samples transactions to form a view on whether financial statements are fairly stated, a forensic audit examines the full population to answer a specific suspicion: has money been misappropriated, have vendors been invented, has payroll been inflated, have funds been diverted to related parties, have the statements been manipulated — and if so, how, by whom, how much, and what evidence proves it? It is commissioned when something is wrong and the organisation needs to know rather than to believe.

The work combines accounting analysis with investigation. Data analytics across ledgers, bank statements, vendor masters, payroll and expenses find the patterns fraud leaves — duplicate payments, vendors with employee addresses, round-figure transactions at period ends, approval chains that always involve the same two people. Digital forensics on the devices and mailboxes involved finds the instructions, the side agreements, the deleted spreadsheets. Third-party confirmations and bank records corroborate. Interviews, informed by the evidence, are conducted to a standard that produces a usable record. Every step is preserved and documented so the report can be relied on by a board, a lender, a regulator, an insurer or a court.

## Lenders, boards and regulators

In Indian banking, forensic audit is also a regulatory instrument: RBI's framework on frauds requires lenders to investigate suspected borrower fraud, typically through a forensic audit for larger exposures, and to report within defined timelines — consortiums often commission the audit jointly. For boards and audit committees, the trigger is a whistle-blower, an auditor's observation or an unexplained loss, and the requirement is independence. For insolvency professionals and courts, it is an ordered examination of transactions.

GISPL's forensic audit practice delivers to the ICAI's forensic accounting and investigation standards and RBI's formats, with the digital-forensics capability that most accounting-led investigations lack, and with money-trail analysis that turns findings into recoveries.
