A great deal of Indian commerce still rests on a thumb impression. Loan agreements, cheques, property deeds, wills and contracts are executed by impression every day, and when one is disputed — a borrower denies the agreement, an heir challenges the will, a customer repudiates the transaction — the case turns on whether the impression was made by the person it is attributed to. Fingerprint verification is the forensic examination that answers that question, to a standard a court or an arbitrator can rely on.

The method is ACE-V — analysis, comparison, evaluation, verification — applied by qualified examiners. The questioned impression is analysed for quality and detail, compared point by point with known exemplars, evaluated to a conclusion of identification, exclusion or inconclusive, and independently verified by a second examiner. Every item is handled under documented chain of custody from receipt, and the report sets out the basis for the conclusion with annotated images, so that an opposing expert can follow the reasoning and the examiner can defend it under cross-examination.

## Beyond documents

The same examination supports investigations: prints recovered from a tampered device, a document in a fraud inquiry, a surface after an unauthorised entry, compared with a suspect's; identity verified against records for a high-trust appointment or an access incident. GISPL's examiners collect on site with the chain of custody beginning at collection, advise clients who have already recovered prints on what remains examinable, and present evidence in courts, tribunals and regulatory proceedings. For banks and NBFCs facing impression-based repudiation, the service is scaled to the regulatory timelines they work to, and it sits alongside our forensic audit and investigation practices when the disputed document is part of a larger matter.
