Finding weaknesses is half the job. The other half is building the controls that close them, and then running those controls every hour of every day — because attackers do not keep office hours and neither does a ransomware payload. Our implementation and managed-services practice takes ownership of both halves.

The practice covers the defensive estate end to end: cloud, network, application and IoT security architecture; hardening of Microsoft 365, AWS and Azure; firewall, NAC, email gateway and switching security; SIEM and DLP deployment; a 24×7 security operations centre with managed detection and response; ransomware detection and response; threat and vulnerability management; business continuity and disaster recovery with live mock drills; and the ongoing maintenance of ISO 27001 and ISO 20000 management systems.

## How we approach it

Managed security fails in two predictable ways: a SOC that generates alerts nobody acts on, and controls deployed to a default configuration that nobody hardened. We design against both. Detection use cases are agreed with you and tuned before go-live, escalation paths are named people with phone numbers, and every control is configured against a benchmark with the evidence captured at deployment — so it is auditable on day one, not reconstructed a year later.

One accountable team runs the whole estate. When the SOC sees an intrusion, the same team that hardened the firewall responds, and the forensics practice is a phone call away. There is no handoff between vendors and no gap for an attacker to hide in.

## What it means in practice

Defined service levels, measured and reported. Detections that mean something. Response that follows a rehearsed playbook. And a board pack, every quarter, that says in plain language what was seen, what was done and what changed.
