When something has gone wrong — a breach, a fraud, a missing sum, a departing employee with a USB stick — the questions are always the same: what happened, who did it, how much was lost, and can it be proven? Forensics is the discipline of answering those questions with evidence that survives challenge, in a boardroom, in front of a regulator, or in court.

GISPL has run more than two thousand forensic investigations for government ministries, defence and security organisations, banks, corporates and law firms. The practice covers digital forensics across disks, memory, mobiles and cloud; forensic audit and money-trail analysis for financial fraud and fund diversion; data recovery and decryption; private investigations; fingerprint verification and employee background checks; and PhishSniper™, our own AI-driven phishing-simulation platform for testing the human layer before an attacker does.

## How we approach it

Evidence is fragile. The first hours after an incident are when it is most often destroyed — by well-meaning IT staff rebuilding a server, by an employee deleting a chat, by a log rotating out. Every engagement begins with preservation: imaging, isolation and a documented chain of custody, so nothing we later rely on can be questioned.

Analysis follows court-accepted methods and is corroborated across independent sources. A conclusion that rests on one artefact is an opinion; one that rests on the disk, the network logs, the bank statement and the badge record is a finding.

## Why it matters beyond the incident

A forensic report is also a map of how the defences failed. We hand that map to the implementation practice so the route is closed — and to the compliance practice, because most regulators now require both the incident report and the evidence that the cause has been fixed.
