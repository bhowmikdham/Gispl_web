A management system is not a binder. It is a way of running part of the business — information security, continuity, service delivery, privacy, AI — that can be described, measured and improved, and that an independent body can certify. The ISO standards define what such a system must contain; the work is making one that fits your organisation and keeps working after the certificate arrives.

GISPL designs and implements management systems against the full ISO information-security family: ISO/IEC 27001 for the ISMS itself, 27017 and 27018 for cloud services and PII in the cloud, 27701 for privacy, 27799 for health information, 42001 for AI management systems, ISO 22301 for business continuity and ISO/IEC 20000-1 for IT service management. We also place experienced information-security professionals inside client teams where the gap is capacity rather than knowledge.

## How we approach it

Two things distinguish a management system that survives from one that gathers dust. The first is scope discipline: a system that claims too much cannot be maintained, and one that claims too little does not protect what matters. The second is ownership: every control has a named person who understands why it exists. We build both in from the first workshop.

Documentation is written for the people who will follow it. A four-page access-control policy that engineers actually read is worth more than a forty-page one that nobody does — and auditors know the difference.

## Maintenance, not just certification

Certificates have a three-year life and annual surveillance audits in between. Most organisations let the system decay between visits and scramble before each one. Our maintenance engagements run the cycle continuously: risk reviews, internal audits, management reviews, corrective actions and the evidence trail, so surveillance is routine and recertification is a formality.
