A certificate is a promise to someone outside your company: a customer, a regulator, a card brand, a board. It says that a named set of controls exists, works and is checked. The work of compliance is making that promise true — and being able to prove it on the day an auditor, or an incident, tests it.

GISPL has delivered more than five thousand compliance audits since 2012, across banks and NBFCs, government ministries, pharmaceutical manufacturers, telecom carriers and Fortune 500 subsidiaries in India, the UAE, the USA, Canada and Australia. The practice covers the global standards — PCI DSS, ISO 27001 and its family, SOC 1 and SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR, NIST CSF, IEC 62443, TISAX — and the Indian regulatory mandates that sit on top of them: RBI, SEBI CSCRF, IRDAI and the DPDP Act.

## How we approach it

Most compliance failures are not failures of intent. They are the result of treating a standard as a document to be written rather than a system to be run. So we start with the systems: where data actually flows, who actually has access, what the suppliers actually do. The gap assessment measures that reality against the standard, clause by clause, and the remediation plan is built with the people who will own each control after we leave.

We are CERT-In empanelled, which matters for the mandates that require it, and we are independent: no product to sell, no platform to push. The only thing we are paid for is the distance closed between your controls and the standard.

## What you get

A scoped programme with a named lead. Evidence assembled the way an assessor reads it. Findings handled rather than forwarded. And a certificate that holds up — at the surveillance audit, at the customer due-diligence questionnaire, and in front of the regulator.
