Universities and hotels have more in common than they expect. Both run networks that thousands of transient people connect to with their own devices, next to systems that hold identity, financial and personal data — the student information system, the property management system, the payment gateway. Both take card payments through a dozen channels, every one of them in PCI scope. Both hold data on people who did not choose them as their custodian: students, many of them minors, and guests, many of them foreign. Both are targeted at the moments they cannot afford it — admissions, examinations, peak occupancy — because that is when they pay. And both typically have an IT team of a few people, no security specialist, and a budget set by the fee or the room rate.

The DPDP Act has changed the stakes for both. A school or university is a data fiduciary for every student, applicant, parent and employee, and the Act's provisions on children's data add verifiable parental consent and a prohibition on tracking and targeted advertising that reach into learning platforms and ed-tech partners. A hotel is a data fiduciary for every guest — passport, itinerary, preferences, payment — shared across booking engines, loyalty programmes, OTAs and franchisors. Add the GDPR for European students and guests and PCI DSS for the payments, and a modest institution is answering to several regimes with a very small team.

## What we do for education and hospitality

Segmentation and validation, first: guest and student networks treated as hostile, separated from administrative, academic and payment systems, and the separation proven by testing. PCI scope reduction — hosted payment pages, tokenisation, segmentation — so the channels that must be secured are few, then the validation itself. DPDP and GDPR mapped to admissions, learning-management, examination, booking, loyalty and partner systems, with the children's-data provisions handled explicitly. Testing of the student and guest-facing applications that are the front door. Ransomware readiness timed to the academic and occupancy calendar. Awareness programmes that scale to tens of thousands of users through simulation and short modules. And, for teams that cannot hire a SOC, managed monitoring through ours — because the campus network is the internet, and something on it should be watching.
