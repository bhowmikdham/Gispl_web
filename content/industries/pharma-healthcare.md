Healthcare holds the most sensitive data there is and runs on some of the least defensible technology. A patient record combines identity, financial and clinical information, and under the DPDP Act, the GDPR and HIPAA it carries the highest obligations any data does. The systems that hold it — electronic medical records, imaging, laboratory, pharmacy, claims — sit on the same networks as connected medical devices that run unsupported software and cannot be patched, scanned or switched off. And the sector is targeted precisely because it pays: a hospital with a locked EMR is a hospital that cannot treat.

For pharmaceutical manufacturers and research organisations the picture adds a regulatory dimension of its own. Security is also data integrity: under 21 CFR Part 11 and GxP expectations, a record that cannot be attributed, that has been altered without a trail, or that was produced by a compromised system is a regulatory finding and potentially a batch problem. Intellectual property — formulations, processes, trial data — is the business. And the Indian companies that provide health-IT, transcription, billing, analytics and clinical-research services to US and European clients are HIPAA business associates and GDPR processors, with direct liability and clients whose compliance teams will not sign without evidence.
