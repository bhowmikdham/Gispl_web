A telecom network is national infrastructure that never stops. It carries the country's calls, data, payments and emergency services; it holds identity, location and usage records for hundreds of millions of people; and its core, signalling and transport systems are designated critical information infrastructure. The threats match: state-level adversaries interested in interception and disruption, criminals interested in subscriber data and fraud, and the operational risk of equipment, vendors and integrators from everywhere touching the network every day.

The regulation has caught up. The Telecommunications Act 2023 and the Telecom Cyber Security Rules made under it give the Department of Telecommunications direct authority over cyber security — a designated Chief Telecommunication Security Officer, security audits, incident reporting within six hours, and binding directions — on top of licence conditions that already require ISO 27001 and security audits, NCIIPC's requirements for critical information infrastructure, trusted-source rules for equipment, and the DPDP Act's obligations for what is probably the largest personal-data estate in the country. GISPL has worked with carriers, ISPs, data-centre operators and managed-service providers in India and the Gulf on every layer of this.

## Cloud-native cores and the next network

5G brought virtualised, containerised cores, network slicing, edge computing and APIs exposed to enterprise customers — cloud security problems inside the network. Our cloud, container and application security practices work alongside the telecom team so that a core running on Kubernetes is secured as both a network function and a cloud workload, and so that the enterprise-facing APIs are tested as the attack surface they are.
