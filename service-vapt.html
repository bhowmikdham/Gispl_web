Four exercises people keep confusing.

A penetration test finds weaknesses. These four answer different questions, and buying the wrong one is expensive — so here is the honest difference.

Red team

ADVERSARY SIMULATION

We behave like the attacker you are actually likely to face, against your live environment, to the scope you authorise. The question it answers is not “do we have vulnerabilities” but “could someone get to what matters, and would anyone notice”.

Blue team

DEFENSIVE READINESS

An evaluation of your defenders rather than your perimeter: detection coverage, alert quality, escalation paths and incident-response readiness. Most organisations discover their tooling saw the attack and nobody acted on it.

Purple team

BOTH, DELIBERATELY TOGETHER

Our testers and your defenders in the same room, running attacks and tuning detections in the same session. It is the fastest way to raise detection coverage, because the feedback loop is minutes rather than the weeks a report takes.

Compromise assessment

ARE YOU ALREADY BREACHED?

A hunt through your environment for evidence of intrusion that is active now or happened already and was never caught. Commissioned before an acquisition, after a scare, or when something simply does not add up.

Before you scope it.

The questions that come up in almost every first call. Anything else — ask, and you get a straight answer rather than a brochure.

How long does a test take?+

Scope decides it. A single web application is usually a matter of days; a full internal and external network across several sites takes longer. You get a fixed scope, a fixed price and a timeline before any testing starts, so the answer is agreed rather than discovered.

Will testing disrupt production?+

Rules of engagement are agreed in writing first — what is in scope, what is explicitly excluded, and which tests only run inside an agreed window. OT and SCADA environments are treated differently by default: on a plant floor, availability is the safety control.

Do you retest after we fix things?+

Yes, and it is included rather than quoted separately. Once you have remediated we retest the findings and issue a closure certificate — a finding is not closed because a ticket was closed.

What do we do with the report internally?+

It is built for two audiences at once: an executive summary the board can act on, and a technical body with reproduction steps a developer can follow. Findings carry CVSS v3.1 ratings and are ranked by exploitability in your environment, not by generic severity.

Do you sign an NDA before we share anything?+

Yes, and we encourage it. We routinely sign NDAs before any scoping discussion — ours or yours.
